What Does a Professional House Wash Include? Siding, Soffits, Fascia & Trim

A Jacksonville homeowner guide to what a professional house wash can include, from siding and stucco to soffits, fascia, trim and exterior buildup.

Updated August 17, 2026 · Jacksonville Pressure Washing field guide

Written and reviewed by Jacksonville Pressure Washing, a licensed and insured exterior-cleaning company serving Jacksonville and surrounding Northeast Florida communities.

Quick answer: A professional house wash should start with the material and agreed scope. Compatible siding or stucco, soffits, fascia, trim and exterior architectural details may be included, while windows, gutters, oxidation restoration, specialty stain removal and detached structures should be clarified instead of assumed.

The house exterior is more than the main wall surface

A typical exterior can combine stucco, vinyl siding, fiber-cement siding, brick accents, painted trim, soffits, fascia, gutters, doors, vents, light fixtures and screened areas. These materials do not all tolerate the same chemistry or pressure. The cleaning plan should identify what is actually being washed before work begins.

Siding and stucco are the core of most house-wash scopes

House washing generally focuses on organic exterior buildup such as algae, mildew-like growth, pollen, dirt and spider debris on compatible building surfaces. Lower-pressure application and rinsing are often more appropriate than treating siding or painted stucco like a concrete driveway.

Soffits, fascia and trim need careful spray direction

Soffit vents, seams, electrical items, cameras and openings deserve attention because water should not be driven into the building envelope. Fascia and painted trim may also have oxidation, failing paint or existing damage that normal washing will not restore.

Not every stain is normal house-wash dirt

Rust, irrigation staining, oxidation, artillery fungus, paint failure, mineral deposits and deeply bonded marks may require separate evaluation. A good scope distinguishes ordinary organic cleaning from restoration or specialty stain treatment so expectations are clear.

The quote should define exclusions and add-ons

Homeowners should know whether exterior gutter faces, screens, pool cages, detached garages, patios, windows and roof cleaning are included or separate. Clear scope is more useful than a vague promise to “wash the whole house.”

A pre-wash inspection protects both the home and the scope

Before cleaning starts, the exterior should be checked for loose paint, damaged siding, cracked stucco, failed caulk, open windows, delicate decorations, exposed electrical items and areas where water intrusion is already possible. Those observations do not necessarily prevent washing, but they influence how the work should be approached and what should be documented.

Older finishes can also have oxidation or sun fading that ordinary cleaning does not correct. Noting those conditions before washing prevents a maintenance clean from being confused with paint restoration.

What should the home look like when the wash is complete?

A successful house wash should leave compatible exterior surfaces visibly cleaner and more uniform where the buildup was removable. It should not create wand marks, blasted paint, damaged screens or water forced into openings. Some permanent stains, oxidation, faded coatings and material defects may remain because they are not ordinary surface dirt.

The best estimate explains both sides of that equation: what the cleaning is expected to improve and which existing conditions fall outside normal house-washing scope.

The correct cleaning method depends on the material, existing condition, access and type of staining. When the finish or product is uncertain, verify manufacturer guidance and test cautiously before expanding the work.

Common questions

Does house washing include soffits and fascia?

It can. The quote should state whether compatible soffits, fascia and trim are included in the agreed scope.

Does a house wash remove oxidation?

Normal house washing is not an oxidation-restoration process. Chalky or faded finishes may require separate restoration.

Is roof cleaning part of a house wash?

Usually it should be treated as a separate service because roofing requires roof-specific evaluation and cleaning methods.
