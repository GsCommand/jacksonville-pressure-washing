Why Does Green Algae Keep Coming Back on Florida House Siding?

Why green organic growth returns on Florida siding and stucco, and how shade, moisture, landscaping and surface condition affect cleaning frequency.

Updated August 17, 2026 · Jacksonville Pressure Washing field guide

Written and reviewed by Jacksonville Pressure Washing, a licensed and insured exterior-cleaning company serving Jacksonville and surrounding Northeast Florida communities.

Quick answer: Green exterior growth returns when the environmental conditions that support it return. Shade, humidity, nearby vegetation, sprinkler overspray and slow-drying walls can make one side of a Florida home show buildup sooner than another. Cleaning removes the visible growth; it does not change the property’s sun, shade or moisture exposure.

Shade and slow drying are major factors

Exterior walls that receive less direct sun often stay damp longer after rain, dew or irrigation. That extended moisture window can make shaded elevations develop green buildup sooner than walls that dry rapidly.

Landscaping can change the microclimate around a wall

Dense shrubs and trees can reduce airflow, hold moisture near the building and deposit pollen or organic debris on siding and stucco. This does not mean landscaping is a problem; it simply helps explain why buildup can be heavier in certain areas.

Sprinkler overspray can keep surfaces wet

Irrigation aimed toward walls, fences or shaded concrete can repeatedly add moisture. It may also leave mineral or rust staining that is different from ordinary green organic growth and may need separate treatment.

Cleaning frequency should be condition based

There is no single schedule that fits every Florida home. A sunny, open property may stay cleaner longer than a wooded or heavily shaded property. Instead of washing on a fixed calendar, watch for visible growth, heavy pollen film, spider debris and areas that remain damp.

Why aggressive pressure does not prevent regrowth

More pressure does not change the environment around the home. It can also damage siding, paint or stucco. The goal is to remove compatible buildup with a surface-appropriate method and then allow normal weather exposure to dictate when maintenance is needed again.

The correct cleaning method depends on the material, existing condition, access and type of staining. When the finish or product is uncertain, verify manufacturer guidance and test cautiously before expanding the work.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the north or shaded side of my house greener?

Shaded walls often dry more slowly, which can support faster recurrence of organic exterior buildup.

Will higher pressure keep algae away longer?

No. More pressure does not change shade, humidity or moisture exposure and can damage sensitive surfaces.

Can sprinkler overspray contribute to buildup?

Yes. Repeated wetting can keep surfaces damp and may also introduce separate mineral or irrigation staining.
