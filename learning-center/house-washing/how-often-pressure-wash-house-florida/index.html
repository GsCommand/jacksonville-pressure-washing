How Often Should You Pressure Wash Your House in Florida?

How Florida weather, shade, trees, irrigation and exterior materials affect how often a home may need exterior washing.

Updated August 17, 2026 · Jacksonville Pressure Washing field guide

Written and reviewed by Jacksonville Pressure Washing, a licensed and insured exterior-cleaning company serving Jacksonville and surrounding Northeast Florida communities.

Quick answer: There is no universal calendar for every Florida home. Wash frequency should be based on visible organic buildup, shade, tree cover, irrigation exposure, siding or stucco condition and how quickly different elevations stay damp.

Jacksonville context: There is no universal schedule for washing every Jacksonville home. Northeast Florida humidity, shade, tree coverage, irrigation and exterior material can make one side of a property soil much faster than another. View Jacksonville service coverage.

Why a fixed annual rule is too simple

Two homes on the same street can age differently. A shaded north wall behind landscaping may stay damp longer than a sunny elevation. Trees can add pollen, leaves and shade. Irrigation can repeatedly wet lower walls. Those conditions can create a need for cleaning sooner than a bright, open property.

Watch the surface instead of the calendar

Green growth, dark organic film, heavy pollen and spider debris are practical signs to inspect. Oxidation, fading, failing paint and irrigation rust are different conditions; more frequent washing does not necessarily solve them. Identifying the type of discoloration first helps avoid unnecessary cleaning.

Material changes the cleaning plan

Vinyl siding, painted stucco, fiber cement, brick and soffit assemblies do not all respond the same way. Even when a home needs cleaning, the method should be selected for the material. The goal is to remove removable buildup without turning routine maintenance into coating or water-intrusion damage.

A useful maintenance habit

Walk the property after the wet season and again before major exterior projects such as painting. Look at shaded elevations, areas behind shrubs, soffits, fascia and lower walls near irrigation. That inspection is more useful than automatically washing every surface on the same date each year.

What to inspect before exterior house cleaning

  • Paint and coatings: Peeling, chalking or faded finishes can react differently from ordinary dirt.
  • Seams and openings: Windows, vents, electrical fixtures, failed caulk and penetrations need controlled spray direction.
  • Surface material: Vinyl, stucco, fiber cement, brick and painted trim should not automatically receive the same pressure.
  • Specialty stains: Rust, oxidation and permanent discoloration may need separate treatment.

When washing is not the whole repair

Exterior cleaning can improve removable organic buildup and surface grime, but it does not repair failed paint, cracked stucco, damaged siding, rotten trim or leaking openings. Those conditions should be identified before cleaning and repaired by the appropriate trade when needed. A clear scope separates cleaning from restoration so the homeowner knows what improvement is realistic.

For a property-specific cleaning decision, inspect the material, existing damage, access, runoff and specialty staining before choosing pressure or chemistry.

Use visible conditions and material limits instead of a fixed calendar

Green growth, pollen film, spider debris and noticeable dirt are practical triggers. Homes under trees or with shaded north-facing walls may need attention sooner than open, sunny properties. Washing too aggressively or too frequently can expose weak paint, oxidation or aged components, so maintenance should follow actual condition rather than an arbitrary number of months.

Common questions

Should a Florida house be washed every year?

Some homes may benefit from annual cleaning, while others may not need it that often. Site conditions and visible buildup should drive the decision.

Does frequent washing prevent algae forever?

No. Cleaning removes existing buildup; shade, moisture and environmental conditions can allow it to return.

Should I wash before painting?

Surfaces should be clean and appropriately dry before coating. Existing paint failure and manufacturer instructions should also be reviewed.
