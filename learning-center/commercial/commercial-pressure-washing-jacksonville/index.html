Commercial Pressure Washing in Jacksonville: What to Compare

A storefront sidewalk, HOA common area, restaurant pad and office entry may all need exterior cleaning, but the scope, timing and stain treatment can be very different. Commercial quotes should be compared by defined work—not by a single square-foot number.

Updated August 11, 2026 · Jacksonville Pressure Washing field guide

Written for Jacksonville businesses, HOAs, property managers, retail centers and commercial-property owners.

Quick answer: A useful commercial pressure-washing proposal should define exactly what is being cleaned, how specialty stains are handled, when the work will occur, who provides water access, how pedestrian and vehicle traffic will be managed, what protection is required and whether the service is one-time or recurring. Commercial cleaning is often less about “maximum pressure” and more about consistent process, scheduling and documentation.

Why the written scope matters more commercially

Residential jobs are often easy to describe as a house, driveway or roof. Commercial properties can have hundreds of feet of sidewalks, multiple storefronts, loading areas, signs, courtyards and shared access points. If the quote does not define the areas, two bids may not be pricing the same work.

Ask for a map, photos, square footage, marked zones or another clear description when the site is large.

Specialty stains can change the job

Routine concrete cleaning and specialty stain removal are different scopes. Grease, oil, gum, rust, irrigation iron and paint transfer may require dedicated chemistry or additional labor. Some stains can improve without disappearing completely.

Compare our guides to oil stains on concrete and rust and irrigation stains before assuming all discoloration is included in a standard wash.

Scheduling around customers, residents and tenants

Commercial cleaning often works best during lower-traffic windows. The proposal should address store hours, gated access, employee parking, delivery schedules, resident traffic and when cleaned areas can reopen. Night or early-morning service may be practical for some properties, but lighting, noise and safe access still matter.

Water, power and access

Property managers should confirm where the contractor can connect to water, whether long hose runs are needed, how gates or roof access are handled and whether a representative must be present. These details affect productivity and price.

What professional commercial service should include

  1. Defined service area: marked sidewalks, building faces, curbs, pads or common areas.
  2. Surface-specific method: pressure-safe flatwork vs. lower-pressure building washing.
  3. Specialty-stain plan: identify grease, oil, gum, rust and other exclusions.
  4. Traffic plan: customer, tenant and vehicle access during service.
  5. Property protection: doors, signs, electronics, landscaping and adjacent businesses.
  6. Water / access requirements: connection points, gates, keys and on-site contacts.
  7. Schedule: one-time cleaning or recurring maintenance interval.
  8. Quality control: walkthrough, photos or another completion check.

For recurring work: compare the annual maintenance plan, not only the per-visit price. A predictable quarterly or semiannual program can be more useful than waiting until high-traffic surfaces become heavily stained.

HOA and multi-property work

HOA scopes should identify whether service includes neighborhood entrances, monument signs, sidewalks, amenity centers, pool decks, curbs or other common areas. Clarify resident notifications, irrigation timing and vehicle access before the cleaning date.

Commercial pressure washing vs. soft washing

“Commercial pressure washing” describes the customer and property type, not one universal method. Concrete may use surface-cleaning equipment while stucco, painted walls, awnings and other sensitive building surfaces may require lower-pressure treatment.

Frequently asked questions

What should a commercial pressure washing quote include?

The quote should identify the surfaces, square footage or defined areas, cleaning method, specialty stains, water access, scheduling, property protection, exclusions and whether recurring service is included.

Can commercial pressure washing be done after business hours?

Many commercial projects can be scheduled during lower-traffic windows, but access, lighting, noise, water supply and local property rules should be confirmed before work is scheduled.

Do grease, gum and rust cost extra?

They can. Specialty stains often require added chemistry, heat, dwell time, scraping or repeated treatment beyond routine flatwork cleaning.

How often should a commercial property be pressure washed?

There is no universal interval. High-traffic storefronts and food-service areas may need more frequent maintenance than low-traffic office sidewalks. Build the interval around appearance, safety, staining and property standards.
