Concrete Stain Guide

Can Pressure Washing Remove Oil Stains From Concrete?

Fresh surface oil and an old absorbed stain are not the same problem. Pressure helps clean the slab, but chemistry, dwell time and realistic expectations matter more as oil moves deeper into porous concrete.

Updated August 11, 2026 · Jacksonville Pressure Washing field guide

Written for Jacksonville driveways, garage aprons, parking pads and other concrete surfaces.

Quick answer: Pressure washing can remove loose oil residue and improve the surrounding concrete, but it does not guarantee complete removal of an old absorbed oil stain. Concrete is porous. Fresh oil is usually easier to treat, while older petroleum staining may require a degreaser, dwell time, agitation and repeated treatment—and a shadow can still remain.

Why oil stains concrete differently from dirt

Ordinary dirt often sits on or near the surface. Oil can soak into pores and capillaries in the slab. Once that happens, simply increasing water pressure may clean the top without extracting the deeper contamination.

A professional oil-stain process

  1. Inspect the stain: Determine whether it appears fresh, old, oily, rust-related or mixed.
  2. Remove loose residue: Fresh oil should be contained or absorbed before spreading it with water.
  3. Apply degreasing chemistry: The product and strength should fit the surface and contamination.
  4. Allow dwell time: Chemistry needs time to interact with oily residue.
  5. Agitate where appropriate: Mechanical agitation can help work treatment into the surface.
  6. Rinse / surface clean: Clean the surrounding concrete evenly.
  7. Reassess after drying: Wet concrete can hide the true remaining shadow.

Why “more pressure” can make the job worse

Trying to erase a stubborn oil spot with a narrow high-pressure wand can etch a bright scar into the driveway. The stain may still remain while the concrete around it becomes permanently altered.

Better goal: Improve the stain as far as practical while keeping the overall driveway uniform. A lighter oil shadow is often preferable to a pressure-etched repair-shaped mark around it.

Can household degreaser work?

Some consumer degreasers can help with fresh oil, especially when used before the stain sits for weeks or months. Follow the product label, protect nearby surfaces and avoid mixing chemicals. Deeply absorbed stains may still need professional treatment.

Oil vs. rust vs. irrigation stains

These should not be treated as the same problem. Oil is petroleum-based; rust and irrigation iron are mineral/metal staining. A degreaser that works on oil may do little for orange iron staining, while an acid-based rust treatment may be inappropriate for another stain.

Should oil treatment be included in driveway cleaning?

Do not assume it is. Standard driveway washing often targets organic buildup and overall concrete. Specialty oil treatment may be priced separately because it uses additional chemistry, dwell time and labor.

Frequently asked questions

Will pressure washing completely remove old motor oil?

Not always. Old oil can penetrate deeply enough that a visible shadow remains after cleaning and degreasing.

How soon should a fresh oil spill be treated?

As soon as practical. Removing and absorbing fresh oil before it penetrates gives you a better chance of reducing the final stain.

Can you use bleach on oil stains?

Bleach-based exterior cleaning solutions are useful for many organic stains, but oil is a different contamination. A degreasing approach is generally more relevant.

Does sealing concrete hide an oil stain?

Do not rely on sealer to hide contamination. Oil should be treated before any coating or sealer is considered, and remaining staining may still show through.
