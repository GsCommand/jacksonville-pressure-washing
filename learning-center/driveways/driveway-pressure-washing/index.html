Driveway Pressure Washing: What a Professional Cleaning Should Include

Uniform concrete cleaning is more than moving a pressure-washer wand back and forth. Pretreatment, surface-cleaner technique, edges, rinsing and stain-specific chemistry all affect the result.

Updated August 11, 2026 · Jacksonville Pressure Washing field guide

Written and reviewed by Jacksonville Pressure Washing, a licensed and insured exterior-cleaning company serving Jacksonville and surrounding Northeast Florida communities.

Quick answer: A professional driveway-cleaning process typically starts with inspection and pretreatment where needed, then uses consistent surface-cleaner passes for the main slab, controlled edge work and thorough rinsing. Oil, rust, irrigation iron, tire marks and organic growth do not all respond to the same treatment, so specialty stains should be identified before the job.

The driveway-cleaning sequence

StepPurposeWhat can go wrong if skipped
InspectIdentify concrete condition, stains, cracks and drainagePermanent damage may be mistaken for dirt

RinseMove loosened debris and chemistry awayDirty water can dry back onto the slab
Post-treat if neededReduce remaining organic shadowingSome growth can remain visually obvious

Why professionals use a surface cleaner

A rotary surface cleaner keeps the nozzles at a more consistent distance and allows overlapping passes across large flatwork. That generally produces a more even appearance than trying to clean an entire driveway with a narrow wand fan.

Edges, corners and vertical transitions still need separate attention.

Can pressure washing damage concrete?

Yes. Very aggressive pressure, a zero-degree nozzle, close-range wand work or repeated passes can etch concrete. New or weak concrete can be especially vulnerable. Existing spalling, cracks and surface defects should be documented before cleaning.

What about oil, rust and tire marks?

StainTypical issueExpectation
Organic growthAlgae / mildew in poresOften responds well to appropriate pretreatment and cleaning
OilAbsorbs into porous concreteMay improve but old stains can remain
Rust / irrigation ironChemical discolorationUsually needs specialty chemistry, not more pressure
Tire marksRubber and heat transferMay need targeted treatment and agitation
EtchingPhysical surface damageCleaning cannot reverse it

Important: “Pressure washing removes everything” is not a realistic promise. Concrete can be substantially cleaner while permanent staining or surface damage remains.

How often should a driveway be cleaned?

Many homeowners clean when visible algae, dark traffic lanes or staining becomes noticeable. Humidity, shade, irrigation, trees and traffic affect the interval. In the South, annual cleaning is a common starting point, with high-traffic or shaded properties sometimes needing more frequent service.

What should you move before service?

  • Vehicles
  • Light furniture and planters when practical
  • Hoses and toys
  • Fragile decorations near the driveway
  • Anything blocking access to slab edges

Frequently asked questions

How long does driveway pressure washing take?

Time depends on square footage, setup, stain treatment, rinsing and access. A basic suburban driveway can be much faster than a large circular drive with sidewalks and specialty stains.

Do you need chemicals to pressure wash concrete?

Not every slab needs the same chemistry, but pretreatment can reduce the need for excessive mechanical pressure and improve organic cleaning.

Will pressure washing remove black algae from concrete?

Organic growth generally responds well to appropriate treatment and surface cleaning, though post-treatment may be used when shadowing remains.
