How we would compare pressure washing companies

The company name and star rating are only the beginning. A stronger comparison looks at whether the contractor can explain exactly how the property will be cleaned and what is not included.

  • Surface-specific method: Concrete can tolerate cleaning methods that may damage siding, stucco, roof materials, screens or painted aluminum.
  • Pressure vs. soft washing: Ask what pressure will actually reach the surface, not merely whether the company uses the phrase “soft wash.”
  • Written scope: The quote should identify the house, roof, driveway, sidewalks, pool area, gutters or other surfaces being cleaned.
  • Stain limitations: Oil, rust, irrigation iron, oxidation and permanent discoloration often require different chemistry and may not disappear completely.
  • Property protection: Ask about landscaping, electrical fixtures, door hardware, cameras, pool water, furniture and runoff.
  • Insurance and business identity: Verify the company you hired is the company arriving at the property.
  • Local proof: Recent work on Jacksonville-area stucco, concrete, roofs, pool cages and similar materials is more useful than generic before-and-after images.
  • Communication: Confirm scheduling, weather policy, access needs, preparation instructions, payment and what happens if an area needs to be revisited.

Match the company to the project

For driveway and concrete cleaning

Look for a process that addresses pretreatment, consistent surface-cleaner passes, edges and rinsing. If the driveway has oil, rust or irrigation staining, ask whether specialty treatment is included or separately priced. Our Jacksonville driveway cleaning page explains the basic scope.

For house washing

Stucco, siding, soffits, fascia and painted materials should not automatically receive aggressive pressure. Ask how the company handles oxidation, loose paint, window seals, vents and landscaping. See our house washing service guide.

For roof cleaning

Do not choose a roof cleaner simply because the company owns a pressure washer. Ask how the roof material will be treated, what happens around gutters and landscaping, and whether the process is appropriate for the roof’s age and condition. See roof washing in Jacksonville.

For gutters

Separate interior gutter debris removal from exterior gutter brightening. A quote should explain whether downspouts are checked, how debris is handled and whether difficult roof or ladder access changes the price. Compare our gutter cleaning and gutter brightening pages.

For pool cages and screen enclosures

Ask how the contractor handles screens, painted aluminum, pool water, furniture, doors and nearby landscaping. High pressure is not the answer for every component. See our pool cage cleaning guide.

Do not choose from price alone

A lower quote can be the right quote when the scopes are equal. The problem is that exterior-cleaning estimates often describe different work. One company may include house treatment, driveway pretreatment, pool-cage cleaning and gutter faces; another may quote only a basic rinse of selected surfaces.

Compare line by line. The goal is not to buy the most expensive package. It is to understand what you are purchasing before a technician arrives.

What makes a strong Jacksonville-specific company?

Northeast Florida creates recurring exterior-cleaning problems: humidity, shade, algae, mildew, roof streaks, pollen, irrigation staining and dark concrete. Jacksonville homes also mix stucco, Hardie-type siding, painted surfaces, screened pool enclosures, concrete, pavers, vinyl fencing and different roof systems.

A local company should be able to explain those conditions without pretending every problem is solved by more pressure. The best answer is often a combination of treatment chemistry, water flow, controlled pressure, dwell time, rinsing and realistic stain expectations.

Common questions

Who is the best pressure washing company in Jacksonville?

There is no single best company for every property. The best fit depends on the surfaces being cleaned, whether soft washing is needed, the service scope, insurance, local proof, communication and the clarity of the written quote.

Should I only hire the company with the most Google reviews?

No. Review volume is useful evidence, but read recent reviews for details about the type of work you need. A company can have excellent reviews for concrete cleaning while your project requires roof, stucco or screen-enclosure expertise.

Should a house or roof be pressure washed?

Not automatically. Stucco, siding, painted surfaces and many roofing systems are commonly cleaned with lower-pressure soft-wash methods. Durable concrete and flatwork can use controlled pressure and surface-cleaning equipment when appropriate.

What should a pressure washing quote include?

It should identify the surfaces being cleaned, cleaning method, specialty treatments or exclusions, property-access expectations, scheduling and the total quoted scope. Ask whether stains such as rust or oil are included.

Is “pressure washing near me” enough to choose a contractor?

Proximity is convenient, but it does not tell you whether the company understands your surface, carries insurance, communicates clearly or includes the work you expect. Use location to build a shortlist, then compare the actual process.
