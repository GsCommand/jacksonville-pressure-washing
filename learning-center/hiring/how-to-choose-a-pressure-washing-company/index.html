How to Choose a Pressure Washing Company

Compare the process, surface knowledge, scope, proof and accountability before comparing the number at the bottom of the quote.

Updated August 11, 2026 · Jacksonville Pressure Washing field guide

Written and reviewed by Jacksonville Pressure Washing, a licensed and insured exterior-cleaning company serving Jacksonville and surrounding Northeast Florida communities.

Quick answer: Choose a pressure washing company that can identify the correct cleaning method for each surface, carries current insurance, provides a written scope, explains specialty-stain limitations, protects landscaping and property, shows recent local work similar to yours and communicates what happens before, during and after the job. Compare price only after the scopes match.

Questions to ask every pressure washing contractor

1. Make sure the method matches the surface

A professional exterior-cleaning company should not treat a concrete driveway, stucco wall, asphalt-shingle roof, vinyl fence and screen enclosure as the same job. Concrete can often tolerate controlled mechanical cleaning. Stucco, siding, painted aluminum and many roofs need lower-pressure methods.

If the contractor’s answer is simply “we use 4,000 PSI,” ask what pressure actually reaches each surface. The machine rating is not the cleaning plan.

2. Compare written scope before price

Pressure-washing quotes can look comparable when they are not. “House wash + driveway” could mean exterior walls and the driveway only, or it could include soffits, fascia, entry, sidewalk, curb and exterior gutter faces. One company may include pretreatment and post-treatment while another does not.

Ask every contractor to name the surfaces and exclusions in writing. Then compare the same job.

3. Verify insurance and business identity

The brand name on an ad should match the business you are hiring. Confirm who will arrive, how payment is handled and whether current insurance applies to the work being performed.

4. Read reviews for details, not just stars

A high rating is useful, but detailed reviews are more informative. Look for mentions of communication, punctuality, property care, the exact service performed, problem handling and whether the result matched the scope.

A company with hundreds of driveway-cleaning reviews is not automatically the strongest choice for a steep roof or delicate oxidized siding.

5. Look for realistic stain expectations

Organic dirt is not the same as oil, rust, irrigation iron or oxidation. A trustworthy quote should distinguish normal cleaning from specialty stain treatment and permanent surface damage.

  • Oil: may lighten substantially but older absorbed oil can remain visible.
  • Rust/irrigation iron: usually needs specialty chemistry rather than more pressure.
  • Oxidation: chalky siding or painted surfaces require caution because washing can reveal or disturb uneven oxidation.
  • Paint failure: cleaning cannot restore loose, failing or sun-damaged paint.
  • Etching: previous pressure damage may be permanent.

6. Ask how the property will be protected

Exterior cleaning happens around landscaping, outlets, cameras, door hardware, windows, pool equipment, screens, furniture and vehicles. The contractor should have a plan for moving or protecting vulnerable items and controlling solution where needed.

7. Compare local proof

Northeast Florida homes commonly combine stucco, concrete, pavers, screened pool areas, vinyl fencing, irrigation staining and high humidity. Recent work in Jacksonville, St. Johns County, Clay County or Nassau County can show whether the company routinely handles those conditions.

Red flags

  • One method promised for every surface.
  • No written description of what is included.
  • No interest in the material or condition of the property.
  • Guaranteed removal of every stain.
  • No explanation of insurance or business identity.
  • Pressure to book immediately before the scope is clear.
  • Stock images presented as local work.
  • Unclear payment, cancellation or weather policy.

Best comparison method: Send the same photos and requested surfaces to three companies. Ask each to identify method, scope, exclusions and price. You will learn more from the differences in their answers than from star ratings alone.

Common questions

Should I choose the cheapest pressure washing company?

Only after confirming the scopes match. A lower quote may exclude treatments, surfaces, protection or cleanup that another company includes.

How many quotes should I get?

Two or three clearly written quotes are usually enough to compare method, scope and price without turning the decision into a bidding exercise.

What proof should a company show?

Current insurance, a consistent business identity, recent local project examples and detailed reviews are more useful than generic claims.

What is the biggest red flag?

A contractor who recommends aggressive pressure everywhere without asking about surface type, oxidation, roofing, paint condition or stains.
