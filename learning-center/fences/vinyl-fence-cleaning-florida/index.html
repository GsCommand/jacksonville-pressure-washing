Vinyl Fence Cleaning in Florida: How to Remove Green Algae and Mildew

Why Florida vinyl and PVC fences turn green, how cleaning differs from restoration and what to inspect before washing.

Updated August 17, 2026 · Jacksonville Pressure Washing field guide

Written and reviewed by Jacksonville Pressure Washing, a licensed and insured exterior-cleaning company serving Jacksonville and surrounding Northeast Florida communities.

Quick answer: Green buildup on vinyl and PVC fences is commonly associated with organic growth on shaded or slow-drying surfaces. Cleaning should use a material-appropriate process rather than close-range pressure that can mark panels or force water and debris into joints.

Why white fences show buildup quickly

Bright vinyl makes green film, pollen, irrigation residue and splashback easy to see. Shaded sides and panels near landscaping can stay damp longer, so one side of the fence may need attention before another.

Low pressure usually does more than enough

Vinyl does not need the same mechanical force as old concrete. Treatment and controlled rinsing can address ordinary organic buildup while reducing the chance of wand marks or damage around caps, gates and panel joints.

Not every stain is algae

Irrigation rust, paint transfer, oxidation, scuffs and embedded discoloration can look different from organic growth and may need specialty treatment. A normal fence wash should not be sold as guaranteed restoration of every mark.

Gates and hardware deserve inspection

Hinges, latches, decorative caps and nearby plants can be affected by cleaning solution or concentrated spray. A complete fence-cleaning plan includes these details instead of treating the fence as one uninterrupted flat surface.

The safest cleaning decision starts with the material, existing damage, coating condition, access and the type of staining present.

Frequently asked questions

Can you pressure wash a vinyl fence?

Vinyl can be cleaned, but aggressive close-range pressure is usually unnecessary and can create surface or joint problems.

Why does a vinyl fence turn green?

Shade, moisture, pollen and organic growth can create green film, especially in slow-drying areas.

Will cleaning remove irrigation rust?

Rust and mineral staining can require separate chemistry from ordinary organic fence cleaning.
