Jacksonville Pool-Area Cleaning Guide

Pool Deck, Pool Cage & Screen Enclosure Cleaning

A pool area combines several materials in one small space: concrete or pavers, painted aluminum, screens, doors, furniture, landscaping and pool water. The safest cleaning plan treats those components differently instead of using one pressure setting everywhere.

Updated August 11, 2026 · Jacksonville Pressure Washing field guide

Written and reviewed by Jacksonville Pressure Washing, a licensed and insured exterior-cleaning company serving Jacksonville and surrounding Northeast Florida communities.

Quick answer: Pool decks and screen enclosures should be cleaned according to the material. Durable concrete can often use controlled pressure and surface-cleaning equipment, while screens, painted aluminum, doors and trim generally call for lower-pressure washing and careful chemistry. Runoff, pool water, furniture and nearby plants also need to be considered before cleaning begins.

Why Jacksonville pool areas get dirty quickly

Humidity, frequent rain, shade from cages and landscaping, irrigation, pollen and constant moisture can create ideal conditions for green or black organic buildup. North-facing and heavily shaded deck areas often darken faster than sun-exposed sections.

A clean pool surface does not mean every stain is organic. Rust from furniture, irrigation iron, leaf tannins and oxidation on painted aluminum may require separate treatment.

Pool-deck cleaning

For concrete, a professional process often uses pretreatment followed by consistent surface-cleaner passes, edge work and controlled rinsing. The goal is uniform cleaning rather than carving bright wand marks into individual spots.

Pavers need different judgment. Joint sand, existing sealer, movement, efflorescence and previous pressure damage should be considered before high-pressure cleaning.

Pool cages and painted aluminum

Painted aluminum can collect algae and chalky oxidation. Those are not the same condition. Organic growth may wash away; oxidation is degradation of the coating and can remain or become more obvious after cleaning.

A contractor should identify loose paint, oxidation, corroded fasteners, screen condition and any fragile enclosure components before washing.

Screen enclosure cleaning

Screens need enough flow and treatment to release debris without concentrating force at close range. Cleaning should also account for the inside and outside faces, spline areas, framing and doors. If the enclosure is tall, access method and safety can affect the scope and price.

What should be moved before service?

  1. Lightweight furniture, cushions and fabrics.
  2. Grills, toys and loose décor where practical.
  3. Items blocking enclosure doors or deck edges.
  4. Fragile planters or objects that could be affected by spray.
  5. Pool-cleaning equipment or electronics that should stay dry.

Large furniture may sometimes remain if the agreed scope allows cleaning around it, but that creates missed areas. Clarify furniture handling before the appointment.

Protecting pool water and landscaping

Professional cleaning around a pool should control where treatment is applied, minimize unnecessary overspray and rinse surrounding areas. Plants and sensitive materials may require pre-wetting, shielding or additional rinsing depending on the chemistry used.

Ask before hiring: Will the quote include the deck, cage frame, screens, doors and outside cage surfaces? Will furniture be moved? How is runoff around the pool handled? These details change what “pool cage cleaning” actually means.

What normal cleaning may not fix

Oxidized aluminum, failing paint, rusted hardware, torn screens, calcium deposits, etched concrete, permanent leaf staining and old paver-sealer failures are maintenance or restoration issues rather than ordinary dirt. They can remain after an otherwise successful wash.

Common questions

Can a pool cage be pressure washed?

Some durable components can tolerate controlled washing, but screens, painted aluminum, doors and enclosure details should not automatically receive aggressive pressure. Lower-pressure cleaning is often appropriate.

Can pool-deck cleaning affect pool water?

Runoff, overspray and cleaning chemistry should be managed around the pool. Contractors should plan spray direction, rinsing and protection around water, equipment and furniture.

What causes green or black buildup on pool decks?

Shade, humidity, standing moisture, landscaping and Florida weather can support algae and mildew on pool-area surfaces.

Does pool cage cleaning remove oxidation?

Normal washing can remove organic buildup and dirt, but it does not restore a chalky or deteriorated paint finish. Oxidation may require separate restoration or repainting.
