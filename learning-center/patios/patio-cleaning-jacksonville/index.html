Patio Cleaning in Jacksonville: Concrete, Brick and Paver Patios

How Jacksonville patio cleaning changes for concrete, brick, pavers, coatings and specialty stains.

Updated August 17, 2026 · Jacksonville Pressure Washing field guide

Written and reviewed by Jacksonville Pressure Washing, a licensed and insured exterior-cleaning company serving Jacksonville and surrounding Northeast Florida communities.

Quick answer: Patio cleaning is not one method because patio materials vary. Broom-finished concrete can tolerate a different process from clay brick, jointed pavers, painted surfaces or coated decorative concrete.

Identify the patio material first

Concrete, brick and interlocking pavers can all look like durable hardscape, but joints, surface texture, coatings and prior sealing change the risk. A surface cleaner that works well on plain concrete may not be appropriate for every patio.

Paver joints need special attention

Pressure can remove loose joint sand from between pavers. If the patio has polymeric sand, sealer or unstable joints, cleaning should consider the condition of those materials before work begins.

Specialty stains need specialty treatment

Rust, irrigation staining, grease, leaf tannins and embedded discoloration may not disappear with general pressure cleaning. The American Cement Association notes that stain-removal methods for concrete should be selected according to the stain type.

Outdoor living areas have surrounding risks

Doors, screens, furniture, grills, pool water, landscaping and drainage paths can all sit close to a patio. Cleaning the surface is only part of the job; controlling spray and runoff matters too.

What to inspect before patio cleaning

  • Surface type: Plain concrete, brick, pavers, coatings and natural stone do not share one cleaning method.
  • Joints: Loose sand or polymeric joint material can be disturbed by pressure.
  • Stains: Oil, rust, irrigation minerals and tannins may require separate chemistry.
  • Adjacent areas: Doors, screens, landscaping, pools and furniture affect spray and runoff control.

Cleaning versus restoration

General patio cleaning targets removable dirt and organic buildup. Replacing joint sand, stripping failed sealer, repairing pavers or restoring coatings are separate scopes. A useful quote should identify the surface and the visible problem before deciding whether ordinary cleaning is enough.

The safest cleaning decision starts with the material, existing damage, coating condition, access and the type of staining present.

Frequently asked questions

Can pavers be pressure washed?

They can be cleaned, but joint sand, sealer and paver condition should be evaluated before selecting pressure and technique.

Will pressure washing remove rust?

Not always. Rust often requires chemistry selected for the stain and surface.

Are concrete and brick patios cleaned the same way?

Not automatically. Material, joints, coatings and condition can require different methods.
