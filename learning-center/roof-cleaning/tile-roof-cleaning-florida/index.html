Tile Roof Cleaning in Florida: Clay and Concrete Tile Roofs

A homeowner guide to cleaning clay and concrete tile roofs while considering tile condition, coatings, pitch and water management.

Updated August 17, 2026 · Jacksonville Pressure Washing field guide

Written and reviewed by Jacksonville Pressure Washing, a licensed and insured exterior-cleaning company serving Jacksonville and surrounding Northeast Florida communities.

Quick answer: Tile roofs need roof-specific cleaning, not a one-size-fits-all pressure setting. Clay and concrete tiles can differ in finish, age and condition, and cracked or loose tiles can create risks before cleaning begins.

Jacksonville context: Tile roofs around Jacksonville and Northeast Florida can collect organic staining in Florida’s humid climate, but clay tile, concrete tile, coatings and repair history can change the cleaning limits. A local roof-cleaning plan should start with the actual tile system. See our Jacksonville service area.

Tile type and condition come first

Concrete tile, clay tile and coated tile surfaces can look similar from the ground but behave differently. Age, surface wear, previous coatings and cracked tiles should be identified before cleaning.

Walking the roof can be part of the risk

Tile can break under point loads, and wet roof surfaces can be slippery. Access strategy is therefore part of the cleaning decision, not just the chemistry or pressure.

Organic growth versus permanent discoloration

Dark organic buildup may respond to roof treatment, while mineral staining, coating wear and color variation can remain. Setting that expectation before the job avoids treating every visible mark as the same problem.

Drainage and surrounding surfaces matter

Roof runoff may pass through gutters, over pool areas or near landscaping. A complete roof-cleaning plan should consider where treatment and rinse water travel after leaving the tile surface.

What to inspect before roof cleaning

  • Roof material: Asphalt shingles, clay tile, concrete tile and coated metal each have different cleaning limits.
  • Age and condition: Broken tile, loose shingles, failed coatings and flashing issues are roofing conditions, not cleaning problems.
  • Pitch and access: Roof work adds fall and slip hazards that should influence the service plan.
  • Runoff path: Gutters, landscaping, siding, pools and hardscape below the roof can receive cleaning runoff.

When roof cleaning is not enough

Cleaning can address compatible organic staining, but it does not fix leaks, deteriorated roofing, failed flashing or structural problems. If the roof shows damage beyond surface discoloration, a roofing inspection may be more important than cleaning. Manufacturer cleaning guidance should also take priority over a generic exterior-washing method.

Clay tile and concrete tile should not be treated as identical

Both are commonly described as “tile roofs,” yet their surface condition, porosity, coatings and age can differ. A glazed or coated tile can react differently from an older uncoated concrete tile. Before choosing chemistry or pressure, identify the material and inspect whether the visible finish is intact.

Broken or displaced tiles are also a roofing condition, not a cleaning problem. If access requires moving across the roof, existing cracks and fragile areas matter because a cleaning plan should not create a repair issue while trying to improve appearance.

Tile-roof conditions to document before cleaning

  • Tile type and finish: Identify clay versus concrete and note coatings, glaze or previous treatments.
  • Cracked or loose pieces: Photograph visible damage before work begins.
  • Valleys and penetrations: Note areas where debris, runoff or previous repairs concentrate.
  • Below-roof surfaces: Landscaping, gutters, siding, patios and pool areas all influence runoff management.

Questions that separate cleaning from repair

  • Are the dark areas removable organic staining or permanent color variation?
  • Is the roof safe to access without stressing damaged tiles?
  • Would a roofing contractor need to address broken pieces before cleaning?

Common questions

Are clay and concrete tile cleaned the same way?

Not automatically. Material, coating, age and condition can change the appropriate method.

Can broken tile be fixed by cleaning?

No. Cracked, loose or damaged tiles are roofing issues, not cleaning issues.

Does tile roof cleaning remove every stain?

No. Organic buildup, mineral staining, coating wear and permanent discoloration can respond differently.
