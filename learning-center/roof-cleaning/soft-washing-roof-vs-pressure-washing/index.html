Roof-Cleaning Comparison

Soft Washing a Roof vs. Pressure Washing

The difference is not the machine parked in the driveway. It is how the roof is treated: chemistry, pressure at the surface, dwell time, runoff control and material-specific judgment.

Updated August 11, 2026 · Jacksonville Pressure Washing field guide

Written for Northeast Florida homeowners with asphalt-shingle, tile and metal roofs.

Quick answer: For many residential roofs, soft washing is the preferred concept because it relies on roof-appropriate cleaning solution and low mechanical pressure instead of blasting organic growth away. High-pressure washing should not be the default on asphalt shingles. Tile and metal roofs still require material-specific judgment, access planning and controlled pressure.

Why asphalt shingles are different from concrete

Concrete flatwork is designed to tolerate far more mechanical cleaning than an asphalt-shingle roof. A roof-cleaning contractor should not treat the roof as a driveway at a different angle.

Ask what pressure actually reaches the shingles. A company can own a pressure washer and still deliver low-pressure solution; equipment name alone does not tell you the method.

What about tile roofs?

Tile is durable in some ways and fragile in others. Broken or cracked tiles, steep sections and foot traffic can matter as much as spray pressure. A tile-roof quote should explain access, how the company minimizes unnecessary walking and what happens if damaged tiles are discovered.

What about metal roofs?

Metal roofs can develop organic growth and surface staining, but coatings, oxidation, seams, fasteners and runoff across other materials need consideration. Low mechanical pressure can still be useful even when the chemistry differs from a shingle-roof treatment.

Soft washing still has risks

  • Landscaping: treatment runoff and overspray should be controlled.
  • Gutters and downspouts: they can concentrate roof runoff.
  • Metals: fixtures, screens and sensitive finishes need attention.
  • Pool areas: water and chemistry around pools require planning.
  • Openings: vents, skylights, damaged flashing and roof penetrations should not be ignored.

Key point: “Soft wash” does not mean careless chemistry is safe. A good process reduces mechanical force while controlling the treatment solution and surrounding property.

How should black streaks be treated?

Dark roof streaks in humid climates are commonly associated with organic growth. A chemistry-first approach treats the growth instead of depending on close-range pressure to erase the visible line. See our black roof streaks guide.

Questions to ask a contractor

  1. What roof material do I have?
  2. What pressure reaches the roof?
  3. What treatment chemistry is used and why?
  4. How are plants, gutters and downspouts protected?
  5. Will anyone walk the roof?
  6. What stains or damage will cleaning not repair?
  7. What is included in the written quote?

Frequently asked questions

Is soft washing always safe for roofs?

No method is automatically safe. The roof material, chemistry, concentration, spray direction, runoff and existing damage all matter.

Can I pressure wash my shingle roof myself?

Aggressive pressure on shingles is risky and not a good default DIY approach. Roof work also adds fall hazards and runoff-control concerns.

Does roof soft washing require rinsing?

The exact process varies by roof and product. Some treatments are lightly rinsed while others are allowed to continue working. The contractor should explain the expected final appearance and timing.

Will soft washing fix damaged shingles?

No. Cleaning removes or treats buildup; it does not repair worn, cracked, missing or defective roofing.
