Asphalt Shingle Roof Cleaning: Soft Washing, Algae & Black Stains

A Florida guide to asphalt-shingle roof discoloration, low-pressure cleaning and the risks of scrubbing or power washing.

Updated August 17, 2026 · Jacksonville Pressure Washing field guide

Written and reviewed by Jacksonville Pressure Washing, a licensed and insured exterior-cleaning company serving Jacksonville and surrounding Northeast Florida communities.

Quick answer: Asphalt-shingle roof cleaning should focus on treating organic discoloration without damaging the granule surface. Black streaks are commonly associated with algae, and industry guidance warns against power washing and abrasive scrubbing.

Jacksonville context: In Jacksonville and Northeast Florida, asphalt shingles can develop dark organic staining under Florida humidity and shade. The correct response is not to treat the roof like concrete; the roof system, age and manufacturer guidance should lead the cleaning plan. See our Jacksonville service area.

Why black streaks appear

Roof discoloration can be caused by algae that becomes visible as dark streaking, especially on lighter roofing. Warm, humid conditions and moisture retention can make Florida roofs a common place for homeowners to notice the problem.

Why soft-wash language is common

The cleaning objective is to treat the staining while avoiding the mechanical force used on concrete. Low-pressure application and rinsing fit that goal better than a surface cleaner, turbo nozzle or close-range wand work.

Granules are part of the roof system

The visible mineral granules are not dirt to be blasted away. Removing them can accelerate wear and permanently change the shingle surface. That is why roof cleaning needs a different risk calculation from driveway washing.

Cleaning is maintenance, not reroofing

A cleaning can improve organic discoloration, but it does not repair cracked shingles, failed flashing, leaks or end-of-life roofing. Cleaning and roofing repairs are separate scopes and should be evaluated separately.

What to inspect before roof cleaning

  • Roof material: Asphalt shingles, clay tile, concrete tile and coated metal each have different cleaning limits.
  • Age and condition: Broken tile, loose shingles, failed coatings and flashing issues are roofing conditions, not cleaning problems.
  • Pitch and access: Roof work adds fall and slip hazards that should influence the service plan.
  • Runoff path: Gutters, landscaping, siding, pools and hardscape below the roof can receive cleaning runoff.

When roof cleaning is not enough

Cleaning can address compatible organic staining, but it does not fix leaks, deteriorated roofing, failed flashing or structural problems. If the roof shows damage beyond surface discoloration, a roofing inspection may be more important than cleaning. Manufacturer cleaning guidance should also take priority over a generic exterior-washing method.

Black streak removal and roof restoration are not the same promise

Organic streaking can often be addressed without claiming that cleaning will reverse age-related color variation, granule loss, repairs or faded shingles. A roof with replacement patches or uneven sun exposure may still show color differences after the removable staining is gone. Setting that expectation before work is more useful than promising a uniformly “new” appearance.

Granules also matter because they are part of the shingle surface. A cleaning method should avoid unnecessary mechanical abrasion. When a roof already shows exposed mat, missing tabs, curling or significant wear, a roofing evaluation may be more important than cosmetic cleaning.

Asphalt-shingle pre-cleaning checklist

  • Condition: Note missing, lifted, curled or heavily worn shingles.
  • Repairs: Identify newer patches that may remain a different color.
  • Runoff: Map gutters, landscaping, siding, patios and pool areas below the roof.
  • Manufacturer guidance: Follow published cleaning limits when the shingle product can be identified.

What homeowners should ask a roof cleaner

  • How is high mechanical pressure avoided on the shingle surface?
  • What is the plan for plant and property protection below the roof?
  • Which visible conditions are staining versus permanent roof wear?

Common questions

Are black streaks always dirt?

No. Roofing-industry guidance identifies algae discoloration as a common cause of dark roof staining.

Can asphalt shingles be power washed?

ARMA advises against power washing asphalt roofing because high pressure can damage the shingles.

Will cleaning fix roof leaks?

No. Cleaning does not replace roofing repairs or correct flashing, shingle or underlayment failures.
