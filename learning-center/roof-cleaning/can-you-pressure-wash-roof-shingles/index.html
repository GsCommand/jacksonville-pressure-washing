Can You Pressure Wash Roof Shingles? Why Asphalt Roofs Need a Different Method

Why asphalt-shingle roofs should not be cleaned like concrete and what roofing-industry guidance says about power washing.

Updated August 17, 2026 · Jacksonville Pressure Washing field guide

Written and reviewed by Jacksonville Pressure Washing, a licensed and insured exterior-cleaning company serving Jacksonville and surrounding Northeast Florida communities.

Quick answer: Asphalt-shingle roofs should not be power washed. Roofing-industry guidance warns that high pressure and scrubbing can loosen or remove granules. Roof cleaning should use a roof-specific low-pressure process consistent with the shingle manufacturer’s instructions.

High pressure can damage the shingle surface

Asphalt shingles depend on a protective granule surface. A pressure washer or aggressive brushing can remove granules and create mechanical damage that ordinary roof staining did not cause.

Black streaks are commonly organic discoloration

Dark roof streaks are often associated with algae discoloration rather than ordinary dirt. That is why simply increasing pressure is the wrong response. Treatment, dwell time and gentle rinsing are more important than blasting the surface.

Roof access is a separate safety issue

Cleaning solutions and algae can make roofing slippery. Working from a roof also creates fall hazards that do not exist when cleaning a driveway. Homeowners should consider access, pitch and professional safety practices before attempting roof work.

Follow the roofing manufacturer

Even within asphalt roofing, products and warranties differ. The roof manufacturer’s cleaning instructions should take priority over generic internet advice. A roof-cleaning company should be able to explain how its process aligns with that guidance.

What to inspect before roof cleaning

  • Roof material: Asphalt shingles, clay tile, concrete tile and coated metal each have different cleaning limits.
  • Age and condition: Broken tile, loose shingles, failed coatings and flashing issues are roofing conditions, not cleaning problems.
  • Pitch and access: Roof work adds fall and slip hazards that should influence the service plan.
  • Runoff path: Gutters, landscaping, siding, pools and hardscape below the roof can receive cleaning runoff.

When roof cleaning is not enough

Cleaning can address compatible organic staining, but it does not fix leaks, deteriorated roofing, failed flashing or structural problems. If the roof shows damage beyond surface discoloration, a roofing inspection may be more important than cleaning. Manufacturer cleaning guidance should also take priority over a generic exterior-washing method.

For a property-specific cleaning decision, inspect the material, existing damage, access, runoff and specialty staining before choosing pressure or chemistry.

Frequently asked questions

Can a pressure washer remove black roof streaks?

It may remove material mechanically, but asphalt-roofing guidance warns that high-pressure washing can damage shingles and should not be used.

Should roof shingles be scrubbed?

ARMA guidance warns against brushing or brooming algae from asphalt-roof surfaces.

What should be used instead?

Use a roof-specific method that follows the roofing manufacturer’s instructions and avoids aggressive mechanical cleaning.
